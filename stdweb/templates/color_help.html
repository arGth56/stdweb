The magnitude STDWeb prints after photometry is calibrated on the stars in your field. For a target that is redder or bluer than those stars, that number needs a colour correction before you compare it with a catalogue (Gaia, Prism, APASS…). This applies to any filter system, not only Gaia.

Why does a colour term exist?

  • Filters are not perfect rectangles. Each one lets through a broad, smoothly-varying range of wavelengths, and neighbouring bands overlap.
  • The detector and atmosphere are not flat. A CCD (and the air above the telescope) is more sensitive to some wavelengths than others — the dashed curve above.
  • So the effective response depends on the star's colour. A blue star and a red star of the same true brightness deposit different amounts of light in the same filter, and are therefore measured slightly differently.

Photometric calibration measures this as a slope k (the “colour term”) from the field stars. Your target only gets the correct magnitude once its own colour is applied.

What STDWeb shows on the task page

The task page prints the magnitude together with the colour term, in the same notation as the photometry log:

Gmag + 0.21 (BPmag − RPmag) = 20.06 ± 0.55

Read this as: “the G magnitude is 20.06, plus 0.21 times the target's (BP − RP) colour, which has not been filled in yet.” Until you know the target's colour, the correction term is missing.

How the correction is computed

The target's colour (BP − RP) is measured from its own images in the blue and red bands, taken the same night. Then, in each band:

true magnitude = task-page magnitude + k × (BP − RP)target

  1. Measure the target in three bands (G, BP, RP) the same night.
  2. Solve the target's (BP − RP) from its BP and RP measurements.
  3. Apply k × (BP − RP) in each band → the true G, BP and RP magnitudes.

What STDWeb needs from you

  • One G, one BP and one RP image of the same target.
  • Same observer, same reference catalogue, same processing mode (direct image or template-subtracted — do not mix).
  • Same night: within ±{{ window_min|floatformat:0 }} minutes; same position on the sky (≤ {{ match_arcsec|floatformat:0 }}″).
  • The target detected (S/N ≥ 3) in all three.

When those are satisfied, the colour-correction block on the task page turns green and the Compute button produces the corrected magnitudes, ready to compare with a catalogue.
